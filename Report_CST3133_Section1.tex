\section{Section 1 -- Traditional Machine Learning Workflow and Ethics (Dataset 1)}

\noindent\textit{This section covers Dataset 1: the CSGO match prediction structured dataset.}

\paragraph{Introduction and Objectives.}
This project applies a complete traditional machine learning workflow to predict CSGO match outcomes from pre-match statistics. Esports analytics increasingly informs team strategy, talent scouting, and performance benchmarking; accurate pre-match predictions can support these applications. The report documents: (one) dataset selection and problem formulation; (two) preprocessing and handling of data quality issues; (three) exploratory analysis and bias identification; (four) model development, evaluation, and interpretation; and (five) ethical considerations and mitigation strategies. We aim to achieve predictive accuracy above random chance (50\%) while maintaining interpretability and documenting limitations.

% ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{1.1\quad Dataset Selection and Problem Definition (5\%)}

\subsubsection{1.1.1\quad Dataset Overview}

The \textbf{CSGO Match Prediction} dataset contains historical professional match data with comprehensive team- and player-level pre-match statistics. It is publicly available on Kaggle: \url{https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/gabrieltardochi/counter-strike-global-offensive-matches}. The dataset file \texttt{csgo\_games.csv} is submitted alongside this report. In raw form the dataset has three thousand seven hundred and eighty-seven rows and one hundred and seventy features; after preprocessing and feature engineering it retains three thousand seven hundred and sixty-three valid records and one hundred and seventy-two features for modelling.

Features are organised into two broad categories. Table~\ref{tab:features_overview} provides a detailed breakdown.

\textbf{Target variable:} \texttt{winner}---binary (Team~1 or Team~2). Class distribution is near-balanced (51.05\% / 48.95\%), so no oversampling or class weighting is required.

\subsubsection{1.1.2\quad Problem Definition}

This project addresses a \textbf{binary classification problem}: predicting which team will win a CSGO match given only pre-match available statistics.

\textbf{Specific use cases:} (one) Team management and scouting---front offices can use predictions to prioritise opponent analysis and benchmark expected win probability against actual results; (two) tournament organisers can inform seeding decisions and scheduling; (three) content and broadcast partners can generate pre-match storylines and tailor commentary; (four) sponsorship and investment teams can assess team strength quantitatively. The problem has a \textbf{gambling-like nature}: like football matches or roulette, outcomes are inherently stochastic and not fully predictable from available inputs. The model provides probability estimates, not deterministic forecasts; interpretability and documented limitations are essential for responsible deployment.

\subsubsection{1.3.5\quad Potential Biases Identified in the Data}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Temporal Bias:} The dataset spans multiple competitive seasons. CSGO meta-game shifts (strategy, weapon balance, map pool) mean that patterns from earlier years may not generalise to more recent matches.
    \item \textbf{Team Representation Bias:} Top-ranked teams compete more frequently and therefore dominate the dataset. The model may be poorly calibrated for underrepresented lower-ranked or regional teams.
    \item \textbf{Ranking System Bias:} World rankings are a lagging indicator and do not immediately reflect roster changes or strategic pivots, introducing systematic noise in the most predictive feature.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{1.4\quad Model Development and Evaluation (15\%)}

\subsubsection{1.4.1\quad Models Trained}

Three model categories were developed:

\paragraph{Logistic Regression.} A linear baseline classifier modelling the log-odds as $\log\frac{P(y=1|\mathbf{x})}{1-P(y=1|\mathbf{x})} = \mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{x} + b$. It is interpretable, computationally efficient, and provides calibrated probability estimates---the natural baseline for this problem.

\paragraph{Decision Tree.} A non-linear classifier using Gini impurity $G(t) = 1 - \sum_c p(c|t)^2$ to recursively partition the feature space. Trained with \texttt{max\_depth}=10 and \texttt{min\_samples\_split}=20 to limit overfitting. Produces direct feature importance scores based on impurity reduction.

\paragraph{K-Means.} Per the coursework requirement for an unsupervised method, K-Means with $k=2$ was applied to test whether match outcomes can be recovered from natural groupings in the feature space. The objective minimises $\sum_i \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in C_i} \|\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_i\|^2$. The result (ARI $\approx 0$) demonstrates that unsupervised clustering cannot separate winners, justifying supervised classification.

\subsubsection{1.4.2\quad Evaluation Metrics}

For classification models:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Accuracy:} $\frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN}$ --- proportion of correctly classified matches.
    \item \textbf{Precision:} $\frac{TP}{TP + FP}$ — of all predicted Team~1 wins, how many were correct.
    \item \textbf{Recall:} $\frac{TP}{TP + FN}$ — of all actual Team~1 wins, how many were identified.
    \item \textbf{F1-Score:} $\frac{2 \cdot \text{Precision} \cdot \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}$ — harmonic mean balancing precision and recall.
\end{itemize}

For K-Means:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Adjusted Rand Index (ARI):} $\text{ARI} = \frac{\text{RI} - \mathbb{E}[\text{RI}]}{\max(\text{RI}) - \mathbb{E}[\text{RI}]}$, where RI is the Rand Index. Agreement between cluster assignments and true labels, corrected for chance. ARI $\approx 0$ indicates random assignment.
    \item \textbf{Silhouette Score:} $s(i) = \frac{b(i) - a(i)}{\max(a(i), b(i))}$, where $a(i)$ is mean intra-cluster distance and $b(i)$ is mean nearest-cluster distance. Values near 1 indicate well-separated clusters.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The best-performing model, \textbf{LR with Feature Selection} at 58.70\%, improves over the baseline by 2.79\%. This must be interpreted \textbf{critically}: 58.7\% accuracy is barely above random chance (50\% for balanced binary classification). The modest gain reflects the fundamental unpredictability of match outcomes---a problem with a \textbf{gambling-like nature}, akin to football matches or roulette, where outcomes are inherently stochastic. Esports literature typically reports pre-match accuracies in the 55--65\% range; our results sit within this band. Despite trying over sixty model configurations, ensembles, and hyperparameter combinations, we could not exceed approximately 60\% on this dataset; further gains would likely require a different dataset (e.g., in-game state, map-specific data). K-Means (ARI $\approx 0$) confirms that unsupervised methods cannot recover outcomes.

\subsubsection{1.4.6\quad Interpretation}

Logistic Regression outperforming the Decision Tree indicates that the relationship between features and match outcomes is largely linear. Over sixty configurations were tried across ten model types (Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, Voting Classifier, Stacking Classifier, and K-Means); the best remained LR with Feature Selection at 58.70\%. \textbf{Critical assessment:} The problem has a \textbf{gambling-like nature}---like predicting football match results or roulette spins, outcomes are inherently stochastic. Predictions are indicative rather than definitive; the model must \textbf{not} be deployed for betting or gambling purposes (Section~1.5).

% ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{1.5\quad Ethical Considerations (5\%)}

\subsubsection{1.5.1\quad Potential Biases}

\textbf{1. Temporal:} The dataset spans multiple years; CSGO meta evolves through patches and map pool changes---a concept drift risk. \textbf{2. Team representation:} Top-ranked teams compete more frequently and dominate the dataset; the model may underperform on lower-ranked or regional teams. \textbf{3. Ranking:} \texttt{rank\_diff} relies on world rankings, which are lagging indicators; roster or strategy changes may not be reflected. \textbf{4. Data collection:} Major international tournaments are over-represented; regional leagues underrepresented. \textbf{5. Aggregation:} Team-level averages may miss team chemistry and role specialisation.

\subsubsection{1.5.2\quad Mitigation Strategies}

\textbf{1. Temporal:} Implement time-based cross-validation; retrain with post-patch data; add recency features (e.g., rolling rating over the last ten matches). \textbf{2. Representation:} Apply stratified sampling by team tier and region when constructing train/test sets. \textbf{3. Rankings:} Prioritise relative features (\allowbreak\texttt{rank\_diff}, rating difference) rather than absolute values. \textbf{4. Data:} Expand to regional leagues; document which tournaments and date ranges are covered. \textbf{5. Auditing:} Maintain interpretable models; conduct fairness audits comparing accuracy across tiers and regions.

\textbf{6. Gambling:} The model must \textbf{not} be used for betting---the problem is inherently stochastic (like football or roulette); 58.7\% versus 50\% is marginal. Communicate uncertainty; comply with GDPR for player data.

\textbf{7. Transparency:} Document HLTV sources, preprocessing steps, and limitations; share with stakeholders (analysts, broadcasters, organisers) so they can judge when to trust predictions versus rely on expert judgment.

\subsubsection{1.5.3\quad Key Recommendations}

Key recommendations: (one) use the best-performing model, LR with Feature Selection, for match predictions; (two) retrain with recent data to account for meta-game changes; (three) monitor performance across different teams and time periods; (four) validate predictions with domain experts before deployment. Do not deploy for betting or gambling purposes.
